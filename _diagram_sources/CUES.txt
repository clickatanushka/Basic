## out/m03_s13.png — narration while this diagram is on screen
    [Board: Step 1: mean = 4]
    Step one: find the mean. 4.
    [Board: Step 2: 1−4, 2−4, 4−4, 6−4, 7−4 → −3, −2, 0, 2, 3]
    Step two: how far is each value from the mean? Minus 3, minus 2, 0, plus 2, plus 3.
    [Board: Step 3: 9, 4, 0, 4, 9 → total 26 → 26 ÷ 4 = 6.5 = variance]

## out/m03_s22.png — narration while this diagram is on screen
    [Board: horizontal line, −1 at the left, 0 in the middle, +1 at the right]
    When two numeric columns move together, we measure it with the correlation coefficient, or simply r. It is always between minus 1 and plus 1.
    [Board: above +1, a small scatter of dots rising in a straight line]
    Plus 1 is a perfect positive relationship: when one goes up, the other always goes up, in a straight line. Like the number of identical books in a parcel and its weight.
    [Board: above −1, dots falling in a straight line]
    Minus 1 is a perfect negative relationship: when one goes up, the other always goes down. Like a van's speed and the time it takes to cover 10 kilometres.
    [Board: above 0, a round cloud of dots]

## out/m10_s04.png — narration while this diagram is on screen
    Now, look at this – rating. Should rating be a feature? [Board: write "rating", then cross it out] No! A customer gives a rating after the product arrives – often at the same time as returning it. In ShopKart, returned orders have an average rating of just 2.8 stars, and kept orders 4.1. If we use rating, the model looks brilliant in testing, but on a new order the rating doesn't exist yet. This is called data leakage. I'm writing "leakage" in big letters so we don't forget.
    One more thing about money. "Profit eaten by returns" can mean many things, so we must define it. We will say: revenue lost to returns equals the total revenue of all returned orders. Our assumption: ShopKart refunds the full amount. We ignore return shipping and resale, because we don't have that data. So it's an estimate – and we'll say so on our slide.
